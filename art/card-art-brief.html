Art commission brief · Vinebound

Card Art Brief

Vinebound is a browser card game of duels in a forgotten, dangerous jungle. Players place cards on a 3×3 board and capture each other's cards by comparing the numbers on touching edges. We need one painting for each of the 40 cards, plus a card back. The game draws every frame, number, and label itself, so each image is the painting alone.

Paintings
40 cards + 1 back
Canvas
800 × 1000 px, 4:5
Delivery format
WebP, under 150 KB
Masters
PNG, 1600 × 2000 px

Canvas and safe areas

Every card is drawn at many sizes: about 60 px wide in a hand, up to 230 px on the board, and 220 px in a detail view. Paint for the small size first. The silhouette of the subject has to read at a glance.

Aspect
4:5 portrait. The game crops nothing, but it scales to fit.
Size
Deliver at 800 × 1000 px. Keep a 1600 × 2000 px PNG master of each.
File name
The card's id, lowercase with hyphens: temple-guardian.webp. Ids are listed in the roster below.
Edges
A carved frame covers about 3.5% on every side. Let the painting run under it (full bleed); don't paint a border.
Number tablets
Four square tablets, each about a quarter of the card's width, sit on the middle of each edge. Keep faces, eyes, and key details out of those four zones.
Corners
The top-left corner shows rarity notches; the bottom-left can show a level badge. Keep both quiet.
Focal point
One subject, centered, filling roughly the middle 60% of the card.

Style direction

Hyper-realistic 2D dark fantasy painting, in the spirit of the card art in Gwent. The world is a humid, overgrown jungle where old empires rotted into ruins, and everything in it is dangerous. Paint it like it smells of rain, smoke, and something dead nearby.

Aim for

  • Dramatic chiaroscuro: shafts of light through the canopy, fire in darkness, mist catching light.
  • Muted earthy greens, browns, and stone greys, with one strong accent color per card (see below).
  • Painterly, textured surfaces: wet leaves, cracked stone, rusted metal, scarred skin.
  • Believable anatomy and weight. Creatures feel like they could kill you.
  • A clear silhouette that reads at 60 px wide.

Avoid

  • Any text, letters, runes that read as words, numbers, borders, or frames.
  • Cute, chibi, cartoon, or emoji-like simplification.
  • Shiny gold, coins, gems, and slot-machine sparkle. Metal is old and worn.
  • Neon glows and bright fantasy-magic effects. Light comes from the sun, fire, or the moon.
  • Busy backgrounds that compete with the subject; let the jungle fall into shadow.

Accent color by card color

Every card belongs to one of three colors, and the game tints the card's frame and background to match. The painting's accent light should agree with it.

Green

Moss, canopy, rot and root. Deep greens, dappled daylight, murky swamp olive.

Red

Blood and fire. Firelight orange, embers, dried-blood red, smoke.

Blue

River and stone. Cold teal water, mist, moonlight on slate and wet ruins.

Card roster

All 40 cards. Rarity sets the scale of the scene: commons are everyday dangers, rares are notable figures, epics are monsters and warlords, legendaries are ancient or divine. The flavor line is the card's in-game text; use it for mood.

Card back

card-back.webp

The face-down side of the opponent's cards. A dark carved wood or stone surface with a single vine emblem at the center. Symmetrical, low contrast, and the same for all cards.

Before delivering

  • Each file is named with the card id, lowercase with hyphens, for example mire-leech.webp.
  • 800 × 1000 px WebP under 150 KB, plus a 1600 × 2000 px PNG master.
  • No text, numbers, borders, or frames anywhere in the image.
  • The middle of each edge is free of faces, eyes, and important detail.
  • The subject reads clearly when the image is shrunk to 60 px wide.
  • The accent light matches the card's color: Green, Red, or Blue.
